Axes

K-Means places k centres and assigns every diner to the nearest one, then moves each centre to the middle of its diners until nothing changes. The features are standardised first (shown in "standard deviations from average"), so order value in AED doesn't drown out discount rate, which only runs from 0 to 1.

Basket rules

Which items are ordered together more often than chance, overall and within each segment.

Baskets from

Support is how often the combination appears in all baskets. Confidence is how often the second part appears when the first part is in the basket. Lift is confidence divided by how common the second part is anyway: 1 means no link, 3 means three times as likely. A rule and its mirror (A → B and B → A) share the same lift, so by default only the more confident direction is shown.

Every cell is a pair of items. In lift mode, warm cells go together more than chance, cool cells less, and grey is about chance. Switch the scope to a segment to see that the very high overall lifts shrink. Much of the overall "pairing" is really "both items belong to the same segment's menu".

Predicting reordered from 7 inputs

Reorder model

Compare five models, move the threshold and put a price on each kind of mistake. The chosen model is balanced logistic regression.

Test set size

A missed churner (the model says "will reorder" but the diner leaves) costs about a month of that diner's orders; AED 700 is the average churner. A false alarm (the model says "at risk" but the diner would have stayed) costs one unneeded voucher. Because a missed churner costs far more, the model should catch churners even at the price of some extra vouchers.

Confusion matrix

Rows are what actually happened; columns are what the model predicted. Green cells are correct. The red cell is the expensive mistake (missed churners); the amber cell is the cheap one (false alarms).

Is this data leakage?

If days_since_last_order was measured after the window in which reordered was recorded, a diner who reordered has a recent last order by definition, and the model is partly reading the answer from its inputs. QuickBite should confirm that recency was measured at the snapshot date and that reorders were counted afterwards. Until then, treat about 86% as an upper bound.

Saved segments vs new clusters

The adjusted Rand index (ARI) measures how well two groupings agree, ignoring the cluster numbers: 1.0 is identical, 0 is no better than random. If the segments were just "recent vs lapsed" diners, dropping recency would scramble them. It doesn't.
